# Config Inspector 5.65.0

## Fewer bends and crossings

- **Access point links fan out cleanly:** the links between a switch or stack and its row of access points (or other endpoints) now run as plain Z shapes: out of the device, across on a track of their own, straight into the access point. Their tracks are evenly spaced and ordered so the links do not cross, also when they leave a middle stack member by the stack's side lane (a test page with twelve access points on a middle member had 22 crossings and two sideways jogs; it now has none).
- **No links that seem to join:** an access point or circuit box no longer sits where its link would line up end to end with another device's link, which read as one line. The box moves a grid step or two aside, unless it is straight over its own port.
- **Straight chassis lanes:** a link leaving a chassis module by its exit lane could be drawn with a slight slant (a lane was off the routing grid). Every stretch of every line is now horizontal or vertical.

## WAN circuits take less room

- **One row when it fits:** provider circuits now sit side by side in one row whenever the widest device in the drawing leaves room, instead of stacking in rows over the firewall's own width.
- **Boxes as wide as their text:** a circuit box is only as wide as its name (a long name wraps onto two lines) and its port and address line.
- **Circuits on bottom-row ports:** their boxes go to the end of the row on the port's side, so the link loops round the firewall and straight up instead of crossing the other circuit links. Several loops nest, each in its own lane beside the firewall, which keeps room for them.
- Across the eleven test network designs, links now have fewer bends, are shorter and cross less often than in 5.64.0.

## Port maps and port inventory

- **Fewer inventory pages:** ports in a row with the same settings share one line ("1/1-49 (49)", "42 up, 7 down"), so a 260-port stack fits on two pages.
- **One scrolling tab per device:** a device's pages of one kind (its port inventory, a port map over two sheets, VLANs and routing) are one tab that scrolls through them all, labelled with the page count. Downloads still contain every page.
- **Member bars match the faceplates:** each stack member's bar on the port map is exactly as wide as the member's faceplate and lines up with it.
- **Chassis cards:** on a chassis port map each module card shows its slot letter, so the numbers above its first ports are no longer covered by the model name (the caption under the chassis names every slot's module).
