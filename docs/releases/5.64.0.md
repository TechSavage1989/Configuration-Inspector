# Config Inspector 5.64.0

## Port maps like a switchport diagram builder

- **One sheet per device:** a dark header with the device name, model, operating system, firmware and number of interfaces, and the VLAN key down the right.
- **Ports coloured by VLAN:** each port is a square in the colour of its native VLAN, with a dot for each tagged VLAN, a diamond for the voice VLAN, an outline in its trunk's colour, black for stack (VSF) ports, and its number below. Routed ports have their own colour and administratively disabled ports are dashed. A VLAN has the same colour on every sheet.
- **Members and slots:** each stack member or chassis slot sits under a slim bar ("Member 1 | JL558A | 48 RJ-45 + 4 SFP+"); a chassis shows its slots two across, as in the rack.
- **Trunks and descriptions:** each sheet lists the trunks with their ports and groups ports by description.
- **Port inventory pages:** the port table now has pages of its own after the port maps: every port with its description, mode, native VLAN, tagged VLANs, trunk, voice VLAN, admin state and link state. **Choose pages** turns them on or off.

## Shorter paths, fewer crossings and the right ports

- **Shortest path within the rules:** a stack's or chassis' links now leave by the edge (top or bottom) that gives the shortest path to where the other device really is, including after you move or place devices by hand. A line no longer climbs over a device only to come back down: a stack placed beside its core is reached directly. Open space above the devices is no longer treated as the page header after the top row is moved down.

- **Stack cables do not cross:** when a member's VSF link ports are the other way round, its stack cables now take opposite sides of the stack instead of crossing.
- **Neighbours without a port:** when a monitoring export reports a neighbour without its port, the link goes to the far device's interface whose alias the switch port's description names (a switch port "Inside-FW" goes to the firewall's port2 "Inside"). Otherwise it ends on the device's edge, labelled "port ?". Two such links no longer merge into one made-up port.
- **Tabs:** continuation pages (such as a long port inventory) show as small numbered tabs after their first page.
- **Tidier channels:** where two links between rows of devices cross only because of the order of their horizontal tracks, the tracks are swapped.
